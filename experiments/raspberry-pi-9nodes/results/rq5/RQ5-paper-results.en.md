# RQ5: Performance and reliability along the complete recursive path

Insertion draft for the final 26-condition campaign. It is not a whole-paper or full-security completion claim. Use the methods and limitations with the results. Exact supporting values are in `condition_summary.json` and `condition_tables.md`.

## Methods

The nine-node physical testbed compared matched native recursion (B0-matched), authenticated serial execution (B1-serial), and authenticated pipelined execution (B1-pipelined). The final campaign contained 26 conditions with five adjacent paired block repeats each, yielding 130 pairs and 260 blocks. Twenty-two conditions compared native with pipelined execution, and four depth-three conditions compared serial with pipelined authentication. Conditions covered UDP/TCP hot, cold, mixed and NXDOMAIN workloads, depth-three large-answer near and UDP-to-TCP fallback workloads, a UDP hot/cold depth sweep from one to three managed resolvers, and a depth-three UDP hot/cold offered-rate sweep at 0.25, 0.5, 1 and 2 QPS. The frozen mixed sequence contained 80% hot and 20% cold questions. Deterministically alternating AB/BA order gave three/two or two/three assignments per condition and 65/65 overall. Order was balanced, not randomized.

Each block used 60 s of warmup, a 120 s offered measurement window, and an additional fixed 180 s drain. The windows offered 30, 60, 120 or 240 measured requests, giving 30,600 measured requests and 15,300 separately analyzed warmup requests. Each mode received independently reset/prepared caches. Cold measurement names were unique within a block and disjoint from warmup names, and hot/mixed blocks had a separate successful prime before the frozen sequence. The complete depth-three configuration retained R1, R2, R3 and the authoritative server. Actual uncached successful paths were checked from wire evidence, whereas legitimate hot cache hits could terminate earlier. Paired blocks used identical frozen arrival sequences. Raw acquisition covered the client, management host, three resolvers and authoritative server.

Valid-answer proportions used all offered requests as the denominator. Correct NXDOMAIN replies counted as success, and SERVFAIL and timeout outcomes remained failures. Client latency ran from exchange-task start to its terminal outcome, including a required TCP fallback, but excluding dispatch lag relative to the frozen offered time. P50 used the sample median and P95/P99 the nearest-rank definition. Reported latency values are medians of successful-answer block quantiles, not pooled request quantiles. Paired latency ratios are geometric means of within-pair block-quantile ratios. The experimental unit is the paired block repeat, not an individual request. This post hoc exploratory analysis was specified after collection and preliminary receipt inspection. Intervals enumerate the empirical paired bootstrap's 2.5th–97.5th percentiles within each condition and describe resampling variability in this testbed.

## Results

### Native versus authenticated latency

The authenticated pipelined prototype had substantial conditional-success latency overhead at depth three and 1 QPS. UDP-hot block P50 medians were 31.69 ms for native and 210.29 ms for pipelined execution, with P95 medians of 93.31 and 511.75 ms. UDP-cold P50 medians were 101.93 and 919.74 ms, and P95 medians were 166.38 and 1,463.52 ms. TCP-hot P50 medians were 39.78 and 239.85 ms, and TCP-cold values were 106.58 and 964.31 ms. The paired P50 ratios, pipelined/native, were 6.99, 9.17, 5.95 and 9.08 for these four conditions. These observations concern the complete prototype execution path and do not isolate cryptographic, TLS, ledger or individual-hop costs.

UDP-cold block P50 medians increased across the configured depths. Native values at depths one, two and three were 51.28, 72.94 and 101.93 ms, compared with 355.43, 635.35 and 919.74 ms under pipelined execution. Pipelined UDP-hot values were 199.68, 201.45 and 210.29 ms. These are end-to-end differences among tested configurations, not causal estimates of an isolated per-hop cost or a general scaling law. At 2 QPS in the depth-three UDP-cold sweep, each mode returned 1,197 valid answers out of 1,200 measured requests. Native and pipelined block P50 medians were 90.99 and 1,241.50 ms. The 0.25–2 QPS sweep did not identify a saturation point and cannot establish maximum throughput.

### Pipelined versus serial authentication

Pipelining showed limited and condition-dependent differences relative to serial authentication. Its paired UDP-hot P50 ratio was 1.071 with an empirical resampling interval of [0.992, 1.198], corresponding to a point estimate of approximately 7.1% higher latency. Ratios for UDP cold, TCP hot and TCP cold were 0.967 [0.935, 1.001], 0.976 [0.957, 0.997] and 0.974 [0.948, 1.000], respectively, corresponding to approximately 3.3%, 2.4% and 2.6% lower latency. These paired estimates are not ratios of the separately computed group medians. For example, the serial and pipelined UDP-hot block P50 medians were 200.29 and 199.69 ms even though the geometric mean paired ratio exceeded one. Tail outcomes were also not uniformly favorable: TCP-cold block P95 medians were 1,609.91 ms for serial and 1,765.29 ms for pipelined execution. With only five paired repeats and deterministic order, these results do not support a condition-level claim of significant improvement.

### Reliability and retained failures

Descriptive totals over each mode's participating conditions were 12,897/12,900 valid measured answers for B0-matched, 2,398/2,400 for B1-serial, and 15,172/15,300 for B1-pipelined. Because these modes participated in different condition mixtures, the totals are not an overall three-mode treatment comparison. All 133 measured failures were retained: three native timeouts, one serial timeout and one serial SERVFAIL, and six pipelined timeouts and 122 pipelined SERVFAILs. Warmup had 330 additional failures, reported separately.

Most pipelined failures occurred in one depth-three UDP-near large-answer block, pair044, where all 120 measured requests returned SERVFAIL. Retaining this block yielded 480/600 valid pipelined answers (80%) versus 600/600 native answers (100%) for the condition. Conditional-success latency was undefined for that block, leaving four eligible latency pairs. The surviving answers' latency must therefore not be interpreted as the reliability of the whole near condition. For depth-three UDP fallback, native and pipelined execution returned 600/600 and 599/600 valid measured answers. Their block P50 medians were 191.83 and 1,662.39 ms, and P95 medians were 312.74 and 4,766.33 ms. UDP and TCP NXDOMAIN conditions each had 600/600 correct negative answers under both modes. No failure within a valid final block was removed or replaced by a successful rerun.

## Limitations

The campaign used one physical testbed and five paired repeats per condition. Sequential conditions, versioned control/input repairs and nonrandom order leave temporal and environmental confounding. Seven invalid setup, acquisition or path/cache-validation execution directories were excluded from the valid formal sample, with their evidence retained and valid prefixes counted only once in the final archives. Reliability denominators therefore cover valid final blocks, not all historical execution attempts. Acquisition retained the undervoltage/throttling flag `0x50005`. Stable power is not claimed, and protocol design cannot be isolated from these hardware conditions. Successful-answer latency is subject to selection bias, particularly for the near block with no successes, and must be interpreted alongside offered-denominator reliability. Empirical bootstrap intervals do not guarantee population coverage at this sample size, and degenerate success-difference intervals do not establish equivalence.

Postprocessing checked byte counts and SHA-256 consistency for 13 final archives and 176,385 members, and replayed client wire, accounting and latency numerics. The archives contained original basic execution-identity bindings for 17,570 successful measured B1 answers. These checks do not constitute a deep GC, causal-proof or fault-suite audit, whose status remains `DEFERRED_NOT_PASS`. Resource records contain only host load/free-memory snapshots before and after warmup, measurement and drain. Process CPU/RSS time series, isolated stage costs and extra control-plane bytes per query were not measured. Supplemental P99 values are observational only: at the lowest rate a block has just 30 samples, making nearest-rank P99 the sample maximum rather than a stable tail guarantee.
